# Past features must respect publication time

## Research question and hypothesis

Can a lagged return feature use an exact session window and stay unchanged when valid future data
or later revisions are added? The hypothesis is that selecting each interval's latest available
version at the decision, then linking a complete fixed window, prevents those additions from
rewriting the past. Missing or delayed intervals should remain unresolved rather than become zero.

Q009b separated terminal evidence from membership yesterday. Q010 is the next eligible dependency
before cross-sectional ranks. It connects the existing availability selector and bounded calendar
to a reusable feature calculation. All returns here are invented synthetic values. The October and
November 2026 calendar labels are prospective teaching fixtures, not realised market observations.

## Beginner explanation and definitions

Imagine keeping dated receipts. A receipt can describe Monday but arrive on Wednesday. A Tuesday
calculation cannot use that receipt simply because its subject is Monday. A corrected receipt arriving
on Thursday must not replace the version actually available on Tuesday. Return records work similarly.

**Session:** a listed exchange trading day, not every weekday. **Interval return:** fractional change
from the preceding session's close to this session's close. **Observed time:** that ending close.
**Available time:** when this version and all its underlying inputs could actually be used.
**Revision:** another version of the same interval. **Latency:** extra elapsed processing time.

**Periods:** number of intervals included. **Skip:** most recent completed sessions deliberately
omitted. **Gross factor:** 1 plus fractional return. **Linked return:** accumulated change obtained
by multiplying gross factors. **Missing:** no eligible record at an exact required close; not zero.
The caller explicitly names a return field; the function does not interpret price levels as returns.

## Mathematics and worked example

For h included intervals with fractional returns r1 through rh, set G=product(1+ri) and R=G-1.
This is geometric linking. It follows by substituting W_i=W_(i-1)*(1+ri) repeatedly into the wealth
recurrence. With no external cash flows, W_h=W_0*G. Returns are dimensionless; 0.10 means 10%.
The inputs must describe a consistent linked wealth convention, including any reinvestment assumption.

Start at 100. A +10% interval gives 110. A subsequent -10% loses 11, leaving 99. Thus
G=1.10*0.90=0.99 and R=-0.01, or -1%. Adding +10% and -10% gives zero, which is the wrong control.
A -100% interval gives gross zero. Under the stipulated wealth recurrence, subsequent factors leave
that wealth at zero; this does not establish that a real inactive security has valid later returns.

| Scenario | Selected returns | Linked return |
|---|---|---|
| November 4 original decision | +0.10, -0.10 | -0.01 |
| Same decision after future additions | +0.10, -0.10 | -0.01 |
| November 5, skip one, same historical window | +0.10, +0.50 | +0.65 |
| Same later decision, one microsecond latency | +0.10, -0.10 | -0.01 |
| November 3 interval absent or delayed | +0.10, missing | Unknown |
| Wrong latest-vintage rule on November 4 | +0.10, +0.50 | +0.65, leaked |

The revision arrives exactly at the November 5 decision. Zero latency permits equality; one
microsecond of latency excludes it. A future extension changes no earlier eligible set. At a later
decision, a newly available revision can legitimately change the same historical window.

## Calendar window and information window

Let prior sessions be ordered oldest to newest. Obtain exactly h+skip+1 prior sessions, including the
starting close. Take the oldest h+1 from that slice and use its final h closes as required interval
ends. For h=2, skip=0 at November 4, start October 30 and end November 3. The November 2 interval
crosses a weekend and the clock change. It still represents one exchange-session interval.

For h=2 at November 30, required ends are November 25 at 21:00 UTC and November 27 at 18:00 UTC.
Thanksgiving is closed, November 27 closes early, and the weekend contributes no invented observations.
A row stamped at 21:00 UTC on the early-close day does not match the required 18:00 close.

For each required end, retain versions satisfying available_at <= decision_at and elapsed time since
availability >= latency; then take the latest publication. Unknown calendar history raises. A known
calendar window with absent observations returns explicit missing timestamps and no numeric feature.

## Implementation and reproduction

src/quant_research/alpha/lagged_returns.py adds lagged_return and frozen LaggedReturn. The result
retains the decision, settings, start/end, all required closes, selected original observations, missing
closes, gross factor and fractional value. Input order does not change selection. Asset and return
field matching are exact; one asset's record cannot fill another's gap. No membership filter is applied.

Periods and skip reject booleans and non-integers. Latency must be a nonnegative timedelta. Identifiers
reject whitespace and controls. Target returns must be finite built-in numbers at least -1. Duplicate
versions anywhere in the supplied batch fail, including future or unrelated rows. Nonmatching close
timestamps cannot fill required intervals. No rounding, forward filling or gap compression occurs.

## Limitations and interview explanation

Each record asserts one complete preceding-session return with consistent identity, currency,
action treatment, costs and wealth convention. Availability must include all underlying inputs and
computation. A field name cannot authenticate these claims, detect backdated revisions or repair
future-adjusted prices and incorrectly labelled intervals.

Q008 can provide an interval's split/dividend arithmetic. A test links its synthetic zero total return
with +10% and obtains +10%. General linking still requires an explicit reinvestment convention; Q008's
cash entitlement is not necessarily settled or reinvestable at the next boundary. Q009b's unresolved
terminal proceeds cannot be replaced with a zero interval return or omitted to complete a window.
No source adapter, cash ledger, universe selection or empirical momentum strategy is implemented.

Future-invariance assumes valid batches, unchanged original rows and honest new publication times.
Malformed/duplicate future rows or a failing input generator can abort a historical query. The pinned
calendar supports only its verified coverage and does not predict realised closures.

Floats are approximate. Nonfinite products and complete positive underflow raise, even if a later
zero factor would restore a finite mathematical product. A tiny positive gross can coexist with a
rounded value of -1; inspect gross_return. No exactness or measured speed is claimed.

In an interview, distinguish event and knowledge windows, derive 100-to-110-to-99, explain revision
filtering and missingness, then identify unauthenticated source assumptions. Repository output does
not prove personal independent mastery.

## Suggested answers

1. The loss applies to 110 after the gain, so wealth becomes 99. Multiply 1.10 by 0.90, then subtract 1.

2. No. The economic date is old enough, but its publication is too late. It can enter a later decision
when both availability and processing latency are satisfied.

3. Keep the exact window and return a missing feature with the required timestamp. Do not shift the
window, forward fill or substitute zero. Unknown calendar coverage instead raises an explicit error.

4. No. Arithmetic linking requires a declared wealth/reinvestment convention. Entitlement alone does
not establish settlement, execution or the ability to reinvest at a boundary.
