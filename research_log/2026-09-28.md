# A decision clock is not a trading clock

Daily learning note | 28 September 2026 | Q006a

## Research question and hypothesis

Can a 09:00 New York research decision remain valid while the calendar correctly represents holidays,
early closes and daylight-saving offsets? The hypothesis is that session-date eligibility, information
availability and executable market time must be checked separately. A core-hours-only decision rule
would reject every intended decision; a weekdays-only calendar would admit Thanksgiving.

fixture work and tomorrow's lookup/decision implementation. This preserves the existing provisional

no prices, returns or alpha are measured today.

## Beginner explanation and definitions

Think of a shop with a planning meeting before its doors open. The meeting can be scheduled for a real
business day without taking place during customer hours. A research decision is similar: choosing a
portfolio and executing a trade are different events. Yesterday's closing information may be useful at
09:00, but that does not mean today's 09:00 price is an available fill.

**Session date:** a local date with scheduled core trading. **Core open/close:** the planned regular
market interval boundaries. **Early close:** a session ending sooner than usual. **Decision time:**
when the research policy fixes its input set. **Previous session:** the latest listed session strictly
before the decision's session date. **UTC offset:** local clock time minus UTC. **Fixture:** a small,
explicit reference example used to test software. **Availability:** when information can actually be used.

## Mathematics and worked example

For session s, define D(s) as decision, O(s) as core open and C(s) as core close. Let P(s) be the largest
listed session date before s. The policy requires D(s) < O(s) < C(s). Its separate core-time predicate
is O(s) <= t < C(s): the open is included, the close excluded. This convention does not model auctions
or promise execution. A daily bar labelled at C(s) is still a legitimate summary of the completed session.

UTC instant = local wall time - UTC offset. October 30 uses offset -04:00, so 09:00 becomes 13:00 UTC.
On November 2, offset -05:00 makes the same local decision 14:00 UTC. Holding UTC fixed would change the
local policy. The fixture stores explicit expected instants, checked against the installed named-zone rules.

| Date | Decision UTC | Core UTC interval | Previous session |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 October | 13:00 | 13:30 to 20:00 | 29 October |
| 2 November | 14:00 | 14:30 to 21:00 | 30 October |
| 27 November | 14:00 | 14:30 to 18:00 | 25 November |
| 30 November | 14:00 | 14:30 to 21:00 | 27 November |

Regular duration = (16 hours - 9.5 hours) * 60 = 390 minutes. Early-close duration =
(13 hours - 9.5 hours) * 60 = 210 minutes. The 27 November session remains eligible for a 09:00 decision.
Its predecessor is November 25, because November 26 is closed. Monday November 30 uses Friday November 27,
whose completed bar ends at the early close. Neither a holiday nor a weekend needs a fabricated bar.

For a prior-session input, define a = max(publication, receipt) and nonnegative processing delay l.
It is available only if a + l <= D(s), in addition to session, validity and any staleness requirements.
A synthetic prior-session value received at 08:59:30 with 30 seconds of processing meets 09:00 exactly.
Received at 08:59:31 with the same delay, it is one second too late. Knowing a session has ended proves
neither timely receipt nor that a later revision was already known. The calendar cannot replace Q001.

## Limitations and interpretation

A planned schedule is not evidence that trading will actually occur. Emergency closures, security halts,
auction mechanics, other venues, historical source vintages and the intended 2010-2025 data period remain
outside this fixture. The hash identifies our bytes; it cannot authenticate the exchange or prove what
was known at an earlier date. Timezone rules are not bundled.

## Boundaries that the next implementation must preserve

Q006b must reject holidays, weekends, naive timestamps, wrong decision times and dates outside coverage.
It must accept equivalent aware representations of 09:00, including valid pre-open decisions. Test exact
boundaries and neighboring microseconds, the early close, UTC conversion and insufficient prior history.
October 29 has no known predecessor; do not infer one outside the fixture. Twenty-session liquidity
requires 20 actual prior sessions with usable observations, not 20 calendar days or repeated stale bars.

A source publication may validly arrive after market hours, on a holiday or over a weekend. Calendar
policy must not reject such ingestion. The daily-feature policy restricts which completed session the
value describes and whether it arrived by the decision. Eligibility loss cannot erase existing holdings,
and a later execution still requires price, liquidity, halt and cost evidence.

For an interview, draw three points: yesterday's close, today's decision and today's open. Explain why
session completion, information receipt and execution are distinct. Then move the UTC axis across the
clock change and shorten Friday's session. State that these examples validate scheduling logic, not alpha.

## Suggested answers

1. Decision validity depends on its local session date and the chosen research clock. Core-time validity
is a separate predicate for scheduled market hours. A valid decision provides no fill guarantee.

2. New York's offset changes from -04:00 to -05:00. The same 09:00 local decision shifts from 13:00 UTC
to 14:00 UTC; keeping 13:00 would instead select 08:00 local on the later dates.

3. November 25, because Thanksgiving is closed. The value must still be published, received, processed
and valid by the decision; being a completed prior session is necessary but insufficient.

4. No. The half-open interval is a scheduling convention. A bar can summarize the session and be labelled
at its close, with publication later. Auctions and execution at the close need their own model.

5. Fail explicitly for unknown coverage or insufficient history. Never assume a missing date is closed,
generate an unverified weekday session or pad absent observations. Extend and verify the fixture first.
