# A request is not a fill

Study date: 22 October 2026. Task L002b: event ordering, fees and missing fills.
Sidiq review status: Not yet reviewed. Synthetic teaching examples only.

## Research question and hypothesis

What may change at a decision, and what must wait for a confirmed fill? The pre-check hypothesis
is that a request changes neither cash nor shares, a fee is charged only with an accepted buy,
and absent fill evidence leaves a requested order unresolved. Invalid event order must fail
explicitly. This follows the October 20 L002a hand timeline; no October 21 lesson package was
found, so no study or reason is inferred for that date. Today defines one bounded contract.

The data are six hand-authored synthetic examples, with ordered teaching events rather than a
historical market period. There is no fit/evaluation split or parameter search. Metrics are exact
state/status equality, rejection of ten invalid traces, and detection of five corrupted tables.
Two runs of a separate per-share Decimal calculation must produce identical JSON. C++ remains
the default for the next component; Python here verifies the contract before that implementation.

## Beginner explanation and terminology

An observation tells us something already available. A decision uses that information to request
a trade. An order is that request; a fill is evidence that the trade executed. They are distinct
events. A later price printed in a table does not mean it was known at the earlier decision.

A fee schedule tells us the amount our model would charge; a fee charge is the actual cash event.
Knowing the schedule early does not mean paying it early. A missing fill price means this fixture
has no execution evidence. Unresolved means we cannot conclude that execution happened by the
fixture horizon. Rejected_by_model means a supplied proposal fails our ledger's rules; it is
not an observed exchange rejection. No_order means the decision made no request at all.

The scope is one asset, one order, whole money units and whole shares. No debt, shorts, fractions,
partial fills, reservation, retry or competing orders are modelled. Starting cash and shares are
in 0..10000. Positive prices and buy quantity are at most 10000; final shares cannot exceed 10000.
Today quantity is fixed at two and fee at three. These caps and units are educational choices.

## Which information is known when?

| Event | Available information | Allowed accounting change |
|---|---|---|
| Observation 0 | Price 50 becomes available; cash 800, shares 4 | None |
| Decision 1 | Observation 50, threshold 50, quantity 2, fee schedule 3 | Request only; keep cash 800 and shares 4 |
| Resolution 2 | Supplied fill price 52, or explicit missingness | Fill if valid and affordable; otherwise preserve state |

Require 0 <= observation_available < decision < resolution. Equal indices are rejected because
we have not defined ordering inside one teaching event. This strict policy is deliberately narrower
than a real timestamp system; do not import it as a universal market rule. Invalid chronology
invalidates the trace. It must not be relabelled as an ordinary unfilled or unaffordable order.

## Derive the contract slowly

The decision rule requests n=2 shares if the known observed price is at most 50; otherwise n=0.
It is a fixed teaching rule without evidence of profitability. The decision must not inspect the
later fill price. No cash reservation occurs; affordability is assessed at the supplied fill.

For a valid accepted buy, let C be cash, q shares, p fill price in money per share, and f the fee.
Notional is n*p. Debit D=n*p+f, new cash C_new=C-D, and new shares q_new=q+n. All operations
have explicit units: shares times money per share gives money. Subtracting shares directly from
cash would be a unit error even if a programming language allowed it.

Start with C=800, q=4, n=2, p=52 and f=3. Debit is 2*52+3=107. After the fill, cash is
693 and shares are 6. At the common mark 52, pre-trade wealth is 800+4*52=1008; post-trade
wealth is 693+6*52=1005. The fee reduces same-mark wealth by 3. Relative to the original
mark 50 and wealth 1000, the old four shares gained 8 before the fee. The result is not a
five-unit trading profit from the newly bought shares, and it is not a market-performance claim.

## Missingness, rejection and boundaries

| Case | Input change | Outcome | Final cash/shares | Fee charged |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Supplied fill | Fill 52 | filled | 693/6 | 3 |
| Missing fill | Fill absent | unresolved | 800/4 | 0 |
| Exact cash | Cash 107; fill 52 | filled | 0/6 | 3 |
| Insufficient cash | Cash 106; fill 52 | rejected_by_model | 106/4 | 0 |
| No request | Observed price 51 | no_order | 800/4 | 0 |
| Position limit | Starting shares 9999 | rejected_by_model | 800/9999 | 0 |

The table resets initial state for each case. It is not six consecutive orders. The fixed fee is
charged exactly once with an accepted buy. If 106 cannot fund debit 107, neither cash nor shares
changes; deducting just the fee would violate this policy. Exact cash 107 is accepted, leaving zero.
No_request is determined from the observed price, even if a later fill proposal is supplied.

For missing fill evidence we store explicit null, not zero. Zero is an invalid supplied price;
null is missingness. Filling at the earlier observed price would invent an event. Unresolved
also does not imply cancellation or permanent non-execution. The fixture ends without resolving
that uncertainty. A real system would require subsequent events and an explicit lifecycle.

## Implementation and reproducible checks

expectations, including both decision and final state. verify_event_contract.py is reference
verification tooling; it is not the new C++ trading component or a general execution engine.
From the repository root run:
`.venv/bin/python scripts/verify_event_contract.py`.

The note multiplies quantity by price. The reference debits one share at a time using Decimal,
then subtracts the fee from a temporary candidate balance. It updates the returned state only
when cash and position checks pass. Hand-written expected status, decision state, final state
and fee must match exactly. Both methods share the policy and may share a wrong assumption.

Ten invalid traces cover observation equality/future availability, resolution equality/earliness,
negative event indices, zero/negative/Boolean fill prices, fractional cash and negative cash.
They fail with the intended domain or ordering diagnostic. Five copied tables deliberately

## Costs, failure cases and limitations

The fee of three is chosen for teaching, not a broker tariff. Missing-fill handling is our
explicit evidence policy. The SEC explains that a last-traded price need not equal the execution
price; that motivates separating observation from fill, but does not prescribe our caps or fees.
The model omits spread formation, impact, liquidity, financing, tax and settlement. It does not
predict whether a submitted real order would fill or how a broker would reserve funds.

The equality rule uses event indices, not exchange timestamp precision, time zones or latency.
There is no vector, generic queue, multiple-asset replay or portfolio reservation system today.
The domain checker intentionally rejects a malformed supplied fill even in a no-order trace.
A future interface may choose a different policy, but must document and test the change.

These six cases and corrupt tables verify a reference against a written contract. They do not
prove the future C++ decision avoids later data. L002d must exercise that component with changed
future prices and a deliberately leaking control. No measured coverage percentage, investment
return or empirical alpha is reported. Q004a/Q005b licence and provenance gates remain in force.

## Suggested answers

1. Only the request record changes to two shares. Cash stays 800 and shares stay four. The fee
schedule is known, but no execution or fee charge has occurred at the decision.

2. Required debit is 2*52+3=107. Equality is sufficient: cash 107 leaves zero and shares become
six. Cash 106 cannot fund the debit; rejection preserves 106 and four shares with no fee.

3. It would invent execution evidence and a price. Leave the order unresolved and preserve state;
missing evidence proves neither cancellation nor that execution can never happen later.

4. No. Today checks a contract reference. The implementation still needs future-price mutations
and a deliberately leaking control, alongside the accounting and timing tests.
