# Validate the decision, then ask for history

Daily learning note | 29 September 2026 | Q006b

Sidiq review status: Not yet reviewed.

## Research question and hypothesis

Can a bounded calendar validate the intended pre-open research decision while rejecting closed dates,
wrong clocks and unknown history? The hypothesis is that decision validity, scheduled core time and
sufficient feature history are separate checks. Combining them would either reject legitimate decisions
or admit incomplete inputs. Yesterday's Q006a supplied the reviewed contract and reference schedule;
today implements that contract without expanding its dates or claiming production exchange coverage.

## Beginner explanation and definitions

A shop can hold a planning meeting before opening. Knowing that the meeting is correctly scheduled
still does not prove that twenty earlier daily sales reports exist. Research has the same separation:
the decision clock, trading clock and information history answer different questions.

**Session:** a date with scheduled core trading. **Decision:** the instant at which a policy selects
information. **UTC instant:** a timezone-independent point on the clock. **Known closure:** an explicit
holiday or weekend inside reviewed coverage. **Unknown coverage:** a date for which the fixture offers
no evidence. **Half-open interval:** includes its beginning and excludes its end. **Lookback:** a fixed
number of earlier sessions. **Checksum:** a fingerprint of exact file bytes, not proof of truth.

A closed day is a known answer. A date beyond the reference is an unanswered question. Returning False
for both would conceal the missing evidence. The new API therefore returns None for a known closed
session date and raises CalendarCoverageError for dates or history it cannot establish.

## Mathematics and worked example

For scheduled session s, let D(s), O(s), C(s) denote decision, core open and core close. The policy is
D(s) < O(s) < C(s). Decision validity requires t = D(s); scheduled core time requires O(s) <= t < C(s).
A decision can therefore be valid and outside core time. Neither statement establishes an executable fill.
The exact close is excluded by this project convention; closing auctions require a separate model.

Let H_n(s) be the final n listed session dates strictly before s, in ascending order. Return H_n(s)
only if n is a positive integer and at least n prior sessions exist within coverage. Otherwise raise
an explicit error. Never substitute calendar days, create a missing bar or return a shorter window.

| Decision date | Decision UTC | Prior 20-session window | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 November | 14:00 | Only 19 prior sessions known | Insufficient history |
| 27 November | 14:00 | 29 October through 25 November | Exactly 20 sessions |
| 30 November | 14:00 | 30 October through 27 November | Exactly 20 sessions |

These endpoints enclose only listed sessions. November 26 is a holiday, so November 27's immediate
predecessor is November 25. November 27 closes at 13:00 New York, or 18:00 UTC. Its scheduled core
interval is [14:30, 18:00) UTC and lasts 210 minutes. At 17:59:59.999999 the predicate is True; at
18:00:00 it is False. The valid decision at 14:00 precedes the open by 30 minutes.

Before the autumn clock change, 09:00 New York corresponds to 13:00 UTC. Afterwards it corresponds
to 14:00 UTC. The conversion is UTC = wall time - UTC offset. A fixed 13:00 UTC rule becomes 08:00
New York on November 2 and is rejected. Equivalent UTC, New York, Tokyo and +14:00 representations
of the correct instant are accepted, even if their displayed calendar dates differ.

## Limitations

This is a prospective 33-date teaching calendar. It does not cover emergency closures, halts, auctions,
other venues or the intended 2010-2025 empirical study. A checksum establishes fixture identity only.
Loading verifies this fixture's relevant offsets, not a fully pinned timezone database. Lookup is linear
in 22 sessions; no performance claim is made. History completeness means scheduled dates exist,
not that observations exist or were received in time. Q001 availability remains a separate requirement.

## Implementation and reproduction

src/quant_research/data/calendar.py exposes immutable Session values and NyseAutumn2026Calendar.
Pass the existing fixture path to its constructor. The loader requires the reviewed file's exact
SHA-256 and checks the installed timezone conversions; it is deliberately limited to this one version.
Even a formatting-only byte change fails. This is a bounded loader, not a general JSON calendar parser.

session_on takes a New York date; datetime values are rejected to prevent ambiguous date labelling.
validate_decision accepts an aware instant and returns its session only at exactly the policy clock.
is_core_time separately checks scheduled hours. previous_sessions returns a complete immutable tuple
or raises. The first date can have a valid decision while failing a one-session history request.

## Interview explanation and practical lesson

Explain the three checks in order: identify the session from the New York date, verify the exact
research clock, then request the complete prior-session window. Keep scheduled trading hours separate.
A valid 09:00 decision cannot authorize a 09:00 fill, and twenty known dates cannot prove twenty known
prices. Use November 25 versus November 27 to show why valid decisions can differ in history readiness.

For boundary questions, state the interval convention before coding. Demonstrate the exact open,
the final microsecond before the close and the exact close. Normalize instants before comparison;
never guess a timezone for naive input. Distinguish known closed dates from unknown coverage.

These are examples of validation and data-engineering reasoning, not evidence of trading performance
or independently acquired personal expertise. Reproduce the example before using it as an interview claim.

## Suggested answers

1. December 1 lies beyond verified coverage. The fixture supplies no closure evidence. Returning a
closed-day result would turn missing knowledge into a false calendar assertion.

2. Yes. The decision checks that session's clock. History needs earlier covered sessions; the first
session has none. Keep these checks separate and fail explicitly when a feature requires history.

3. The half-open core predicate returns False at the exact close. It does not model auctions or fill
prices. A completed daily bar can still carry the close timestamp and become available later.

4. An aware datetime identifies an instant, not a universal calendar date. Convert through UTC into
New York before identifying its session. A +14:00 representation can display the next local date.

5. No. The calendar establishes the scheduled window only. Every required value must exist, belong
to the correct session and pass publication/receipt, processing-latency and any staleness checks.
